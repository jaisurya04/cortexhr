# "Think for yourself" answers (Tasks 11 to 15)

Short versions of the answers written in the notebooks, so a reviewer can find them without opening 15 notebooks.

**Task 11: Would you rank on years of experience alone? What groups might that disadvantage?** No. Years alone favours people with long, uninterrupted careers and disadvantages career changers, people returning after a break (more often women) and younger candidates; it also says nothing about which skills were used. In the engine experience is one capped part of the score (20%), and notebook 03 shows how weak "experience only" is as a ranker. (Notebook 02, stage on experience.)

**Task 12: If fixing the gap lowers accuracy a little, who decides the trade-off? Why is equal accuracy not equal treatment?** The decision is not the developer's alone: the business owner of the hiring process decides together with HR, legal and, ideally, employee representatives, after seeing the numbers in plain words. Here the cost was tiny (NDCG@5 0.926 to 0.923), and a screener that reacts to protected details should not ship whatever the accuracy gain. Equal accuracy is not equal treatment because accuracy describes a group of decisions while treatment describes one decision: a small name-driven shift moves some individuals across the shortlist line even when average accuracy is unchanged. The twin test measures treatment. (Notebook 06; `docs/fairness/fairness_statement.md`.)

**Task 13: If v2 is only slightly better but much harder to explain, would you ship it?** Not by itself. A small gain inside the noise of five folds is not evidence of a better model, and a boosted ensemble is harder to explain to HR and to audit. I would ship the simpler logistic model for decisions and keep v2 as a challenger until a gain with a confidence interval above zero is shown on new data. In this project the paired fold difference was +0.0070 ROC-AUC with a 95% interval of [-0.0022, +0.0162], so the gain is not proven and the real LightGBM challenger (CV ROC-AUC 0.692) did not change that. (Notebook 09; `docs/model_cards/attrition_v2.md`.)

**Task 14: A reason mentions "tenure". Could that be unfair to new joiners? How would you phrase it?** Yes, it can read as a judgement of new joiners. The first wording ("The time at the company so far (151 months)") was changed: years for 12 months or more, "A short time at the company so far" under 12 months, and the tail "this reflects a common pattern at this stage of service, not the person's performance". For new joiners the sentence therefore describes a pattern in the history, not a fault of the person. Every row also carries the human-review note. (Notebook 11, manual review of 10 rows; `reports/task14/manual_review_10_rows.csv`.)

**Task 15: If women in the data were paid less in the past, should the model copy that?** No. A model trained on past offers learns whatever pattern is in them, including one caused by a career-gap penalty. What I did: never use gender or age as inputs; exclude career gap (not a job requirement and a proxy for gender); measure what remains with the supplied `group_gap` helper and a bootstrap interval; present a low / mid / high range so the manager decides inside it; and state that the target itself may contain past bias. Before real use I would re-base the target (for example only recent offers after a pay review, or audit and correct historical offers), review any remaining gap with HR and legal, and monitor offers against the band by gender every quarter. (Notebook 14; `docs/limitations.md`.)
